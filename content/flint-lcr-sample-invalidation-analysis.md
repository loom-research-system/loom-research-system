# Flint LCR Sample Invalidation and the 100-to-60 Revision

## Scope

This memo tests the narrower forensic question that follows the documented Original LCR → Revised LCR → FOIA sequence:

> What can the documentary record establish about the two excluded samples and the change in the reported minimum sample count from 100 to 60?

The analysis distinguishes what is established by the captured primary records from what is established by the later EPA program review. It does not infer improper intent from the existence of the revision alone.

## 1. The original and revised forms

The captured original Flint Lead and Copper Report is dated **July 28, 2015**. Its form shows a monitoring period of January 1–June 30, 2015. The form records a population of 99,763 and handwritten changes to the summary fields: the minimum number of samples is marked from **100 to 60**, and the number of samples taken is marked from **71 to 69**. Its comments state that 175 sample bottles and instructions were distributed, that the city had not reached the minimum number for the monitoring period, and that it was continuing to solicit samples.

The captured revised form is dated **August 20, 2015**. It reports **60** as the minimum number of samples required and **69** samples taken. Its comments state: **"Revised report after conference call with DEQ staff. Two samples were removed from list for not meeting sample criteria, and due to population the number of samples required was reduced to 60."** The form also records that not all samples were from Tier 1 sites.

The images of both forms are preserved in the research source set; the parsed text is incomplete because the PDFs are image-based.

## 2. The conference call is independently documented

The September 28, 2015 MDEQ FOIA appeal response states that the September 14 production contained a copy of the Flint LCR and emails discussing the Flint LCR. It identifies the August 20, 2015 Lead and Copper Report and states that, although the form did not identify the DEQ staff participating in the conference call, DEQ believed the call occurred between **July 28 and August 20, 2015**. The same response states that the only information then available to the requester about the rationale for invalidating two samples consisted of handwritten notes on the draft LCR form and two pages of laboratory sample reports.

This independently corroborates the existence and timing of the conference call and the fact that two samples were the subject of an invalidation decision.

## 3. The 100-to-60 change was not itself evidence of improper reduction

The later EPA 2016 Michigan Drinking Water Program Review provides the critical correction to the initial narrative. It states that Flint's population for the January–June 2015 period was **99,763** and that, based on that population, the LCR required **60 samples**. EPA noted that it was unclear when the population change took effect in the state system, but later documentation showed the population change had been documented in an MDEQ email dated April 1, 2015; the state database was not updated until July 9, 2015.

Accordingly, Loom should **not** describe the 100-to-60 change as a retroactive reduction of the regulatory requirement without qualification. The documentary record supports a more precise finding:

> The original reporting form carried a 100-sample requirement, which was handwritten down to 60; the revised report reported 60. A subsequent EPA program review concluded that a 99,763-person population meant 60 samples were required, while also finding that MDEQ's records did not clearly document when the population change took effect in the relevant system.

This is an important example of why the forensic chain must separate an apparent change in the record from the underlying regulatory question.

## 4. The two excluded samples are substantially more consequential

The EPA 2016 Michigan Program Review provides the strongest authoritative retrospective analysis located in this investigation. It states that MDEQ's file lacked proper documentation of the decisions and rationale for invalidating samples from the January–June 2015 monitoring period. Two samples from the initial list of 71 were excluded from the final 90th-percentile calculation, and **both excluded sites were above the lead action level**.

The EPA review identifies the two sites and the documented reasons:

1. **Grand Traverse Avenue:** documentation indicated the site was a business and therefore did not meet the Tier 1 requirement for a single-family residence.
2. **Browning Avenue:** documentation for the February 18, 2015 sample indicated that the site had a whole-house filter.

EPA then identified unresolved problems with the Browning Avenue rationale. Additional samples from the same site were also excluded. EPA noted that the record did not establish whether the whole-house filter was designed to remove inorganic contaminants, and therefore did not provide enough information to determine whether the filter justified invalidation. EPA further identified an April 2 sample collected at a basement tap before the filter for which there was no documentation explaining why it was excluded.

EPA modeled four possible scenarios depending on the filter's function and the location/use of the basement tap. The resulting 90th-percentile lead values were **11, 13, 18, and 20 ppb**. Thus, under two of the four scenarios, the result would have exceeded the 15 ppb lead action level.

The EPA review's conclusion was not that the two samples were necessarily invalid, but that MDEQ's file lacked the documentation needed to establish the decisions and rationale adequately. It recommended a process for documenting and approving sample invalidations.

## 5. What the combined record establishes

### Supported finding A — documentary sequence

The original report was submitted with a 100-to-60 handwritten change and a 71-to-69 handwritten change. The later August 20 report states that it was revised after a DEQ conference call, that two samples were removed for not meeting sample criteria, and that the required number was reduced to 60. The September 28 FOIA appeal independently places the conference call between July 28 and August 20 and identifies the invalidation rationale as a requested record.

**Status:** source-backed / cross-verified documentary reconstruction.

### Supported finding B — the two excluded samples were above the lead action level

The EPA 2016 program review states that both excluded sites were above the lead action level and that the initial 71-sample set produced a 90th percentile above the action level.

**Status:** source-backed by authoritative retrospective review.

### Supported finding C — MDEQ's documentation of the invalidations was inadequate

The EPA 2016 program review explicitly states that MDEQ's file did not contain proper documentation of the decisions and rationale for the invalidations. For the Browning Avenue site, EPA identified multiple unresolved factual scenarios that could change the 90th-percentile result, including scenarios in which the result exceeded the action level.

**Status:** source-backed by authoritative retrospective review.

### Supported finding D — the 100-to-60 change has a documented population basis, but its timing was poorly documented

EPA concluded that the 99,763 population corresponded to a 60-sample requirement, while noting that the timing of the population change in the state system was unclear and that the state database was not updated until July 9, 2015. The captured August 20 form itself attributes the change to population.

**Status:** source-backed, with an unresolved administrative-timing question.

## 6. What Loom should not claim

The current evidence does **not** establish that MDEQ intentionally removed the samples because they were high, nor does it establish that the invalidations were legally impermissible in every possible interpretation.

It does establish something narrower and more defensible: **two above-action-level samples were excluded; the contemporaneous documentary record did not adequately preserve the rationale and approval for those exclusions; and the later EPA review found that at least one exclusion involved unresolved factual questions capable of changing the compliance result.**

The distinction matters. The forensic finding is about the **quality and completeness of the institutional decision record**, not an unsupported assertion of motive.

## 7. Remaining research question

The next evidentiary task is to retrieve and inspect the two laboratory reports and the underlying MDEQ handwritten notes referenced in the September 28 FOIA response. Those records may allow Loom to determine exactly which sample results corresponded to the two invalidations and whether the contemporaneous rationale can be reconstructed more completely.

Until those records are examined, the appropriate Loom status is:

**Documentary reconstruction: supported.**  
**Sample-invalidation rationale: incompletely documented; independently flagged by EPA for inadequate documentation.**  
**Intentional manipulation: not established.**
