CASE 006 · WATER GOVERNANCE · VALIDATED

Flint Water Crisis: The Revised Report

How did a routine water-quality report change after a conference call with Michigan environmental officials?

RESEARCH STATUSSupported documentary sequenceHuman-reviewed · cross-verified primary-source chain

01 · THE QUESTION

What was supposed to happen?

Under the federal Lead and Copper Rule (LCR), water systems are required to collect a specified number of water samples from high-risk homes. The results determine whether the system must implement corrosion-control treatment. The process is designed around defined sampling criteria and regulatory review.

02 · WHAT HAPPENED IN THE RECORD

The report changed.

In 2015, the City of Flint submitted an initial Lead and Copper Report to the Michigan Department of Environmental Quality (MDEQ). The original report stated that 175 sample bottles and instructions had been distributed to city residents and that the city was still soliciting samples because it had not reached the minimum number for the monitoring period.

A later version of the report states that it was revised after a conference call with DEQ staff. The revised report removed two samples for not meeting sample criteria, reduced the required sample count to 60, and reported 69 samples as taken.

The documentary record therefore contains a traceable sequence: Original LCR → DEQ conference call → Revised LCR → FOIA record documenting the underlying record.

03 · WHERE DID THE RECORD CHANGE?

The divergence is visible in the procedural record.

The material finding is not that the revision was necessarily improper. It is that the official record changed in identifiable ways after a documented conference call: two samples were removed, the required count changed, and the reported sample total changed. The later FOIA record independently preserves the existence and timing of the records surrounding that sequence.

04 · WHAT LOOM CAN AND CANNOT CONCLUDE

Separate the documentary finding from the unanswered question.

Supported: The documentary record shows that the original LCR report was followed by a revised report that identified two removed samples, changed the required sample count, and reported a different sample total. The revised report itself attributes the revision to a conference call with DEQ staff, and the FOIA record independently documents the related LCR records and request.

Not claimed: Loom does not conclude from this sequence alone that the revision was fraudulent, improper, or substantively unjustified. Determining whether the two samples were correctly excluded requires analysis of the specific sample criteria and the underlying sample records.
