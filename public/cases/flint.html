CASE 006 · WATER GOVERNANCE

What can the record establish about Flint's water crisis—and how did institutions respond?

Flint is a central Loom case for examining implementation, technical information, institutional responsibility, and correction. The public research record is currently in a source-and-provenance audit, so this page does not present the earlier draft's conclusions as independently verified findings.

RESEARCH STATUSSource retrieval and provenance audit requiredNot yet a verified public case artifact

01 · THE QUESTION

Trace the path from decision to outcome.

The investigation asks how water-source decisions, treatment requirements, institutional responsibilities, technical information, and corrective action interacted over time. The purpose is to reconstruct the mechanism from documentary evidence, not to assume a single cause or assign blame before the record has been tested.

02 · THE CLAIM TO TEST

Was corrosion control in place at the outset of the April 2014 switch?

This is the core candidate claim carried forward from the earlier draft. It is not promoted by this page. The exact source passages, source versions, document locations, and relevant counterevidence must be retrieved and checked before Loom assigns a stronger evidentiary status.

03 · THE SOURCE LEADS

Start with the documents, not the previous summary.

The current audit checklist identifies three leads for retrieval: the Flint Water Advisory Task Force final report; an EPA Region 5 review or memorandum concerning Flint water and corrosion control; and a Michigan Department of Environmental Quality public statement about water safety. Each lead must be matched to the actual document and its context before any quotation or conclusion is relied upon.
