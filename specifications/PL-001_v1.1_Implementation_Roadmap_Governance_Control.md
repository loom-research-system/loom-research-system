## Core Directive

> **We do not add capability until the record of how that capability is supposed to behave exists. We do not call it working until the validator and auditors can demonstrate that it behaves that way.**

This document governs the transition from specification to implementation. No conceptual changes to PL-307 will be made merely to make an artifact pass. Any newly discovered defect during implementation becomes a tracked erratum. The specification is frozen; the machinery must now be built to enforce it.

---

## Interim Governance Rule (Bootstrap Clause)

Retiring the solo-founder exception before the Phase 5 addendum is ratified creates a sequencing risk: Phase 6 requires real artifacts, Phase 7 requires auditors, and Phase 9 prohibits publication before Phase 8 — but the auditor pool and independence rules may not yet exist.

**Interim rule (effective until Phase 5 addendum is ratified):**

- Founder-led artifacts may advance to `accepted` only with **at least two designated internal auditors** recorded as provenance events.
- `canonical` promotion remains **blocked** until the external audit model is defined in the Phase 5 addendum.
- Internal auditors are defined as individuals who have signed the Loom auditor agreement but who are not the founder and are not directly managed by the founder.
- This interim rule is **automatically superseded** by the Phase 5 addendum upon ratification; no separate deprecation step is required.

This preserves the spirit of multi-actor governance while preventing deadlock during the bootstrap period.

## Phase 0: Freeze the Epistemic Baseline

- [x] PL-307 v1.0 is locked as the governing claim-state specification.
- [ ] Establish a versioned specification manifest at **`specifications/MANIFEST.yaml`** in the repository root. Every artifact must explicitly reference the exact specification version used (e.g., `loom_spec_version: "1.0"`).
- [ ] Any newly discovered defect during implementation is logged as an open issue/erratum in `diary/failures/` and `errata/`, not a silent spec patch.
- [ ] **Import v0.5 open questions as tracked errata.** The following items were flagged in PL-307 v0.5 §30 and must be explicitly carried forward as open issues, not silently dropped by the v1.0 lock:
  1. Dispositive-document edge cases (does the carve-out create a low-quality single-source loophole?)
  2. Source-assessment calibration (are `high/moderate/low/unknown` ratings auditable without a formalized rubric?)
  3. Semantic-similarity tuning for passage-level vs. document-level independence checking (§8).
  4. Search-protocol formalization (`search_protocol_id` needs a concrete template to prevent `unknown` from becoming a dump).

## Phase 2: Define the Machine-Readable Object Model

- [ ] Draft JSON/YAML schemas for all core objects:
  - Research Request, Research Protocol, Source, Evidence, Observation, Claim, Verification, Review, Diagnostic, Artifact, Correction, Provenance Event, Actor.
- [ ] Explicitly resolve remaining audit-identified ambiguities within the schema definitions:
  - Claim independence rules.
  - Retrieved vs. merely discovered sources.
  - `unknown` vs. `unresolved` state boundaries.
  - Primary applicability enforcement.
  - Materiality definitions for claims and evidence.
  - Scope alignment constraints.
  - Dispositive-document claim handling.
  - Actor identity and reviewer separation.
  - Correction/demotion event structures.
- [ ] **Resolve residual ambiguity: `unknown` + empty evidence.** Define an explicit rule for negative/search-exhaustion claims. Either:
  - Introduce a `search_exhausted` verification level distinct from `cross_verified`, OR
  - Forbid `cross_verified` when `related_evidence` is empty, regardless of `epistemic_status`.
  - The search protocol execution must be recorded as an event, not inferred from the claim state.
- [ ] **Resolve residual ambiguity: `dispositive_document` guardrails.** To prevent the single-source carve-out from becoming a low-quality loophole, the schema must require:
  - Explicit `dispositive_document` justification field (why is this claim purely about document content?).
  - Subject-scope narrowness check (the claim must be strictly about the content of a specific document, not about causal or evaluative questions the document happens to address).
  - Prohibition against using `dispositive_document` for causal, interpretive, or evaluative claims.

## Phase 3: Build the Validator Before the Research Engine

- [ ] Implement a strict, machine-enforceable validator that rejects invalid states.
- [ ] Validator must output clear, actionable errors: **what is wrong → why it is wrong → what must change.**
- [ ] Enforce rule codes (e.g., `KNOWN-001`, `EPISTEMIC-004`, `VERIFICATION-001`, `CONTRADICTION-001`, `PROMOTION-001`, `PROVENANCE-001`, `SCOPE-001`).
- [ ] **Hard Constraint:** No human override for core epistemic violations.
- [ ] **Mandatory CI Enforcement:** The validator and adversarial fixture suite MUST run automatically on any change attempting to enter the canonical research layer. A change that fails validation **cannot be promoted, even by human governance action.** Without this, the validator is advisory; with it, the validator is a gate.

---

## Phase 4: Build Adversarial Fixtures

- [ ] Before processing real research, deliberately generate and validate bad objects. The validator must successfully reject:
  - Fabricated or unretrieved sources.
  - Duplicate sources masquerading as independent.
  - **Same-source evidence counted as independent corroboration** (the highest-severity defect from REQ-006 v2.0: `cross_verified` claimed with multiple evidence objects all deriving from a single source cluster).
  - AI-extracted evidence incorrectly attributed or unconfirmed.
  - Claims broader than their supporting evidence.
  - `known` status with unresolved contradictions.
  - Canonical claims missing promotion events.
  - Diagnostics supported by material unresolved claims.
  - Synthetic reviewer attestations.
  - Phantom evidence objects.
  - Stale specification references.
  - Claims violating independence rules.
  - `dispositive_document` claims used for causal/evaluative propositions.
  - `unknown` claims with `cross_verified` and empty evidence.
- [ ] **Test Coverage Threshold:** The adversarial fixture suite MUST include:
  - At least one fixture per invalid state enumerated in PL-307 §21.
  - All REQ-006 v2.0 failure classes as explicit fixtures.
  - Valid-state fixtures for every promoted state used in Phases 6–9.
- [ ] *Note: The REQ-006 v2.0 audit failures serve as the primary seed for these adversarial test cases.*

## Phase 5: Build the Human Audit Layer

- [ ] Formalize auditor roles without pretending this is a formal Institutional Review Board (IRB).
- [ ] Define the governance model: *"Loom is founder-led, but its research artifacts may receive independent human audit from designated auditors. Audit participation, scope, and decisions are recorded as provenance events."*
- [ ] **Distinguish auditor categories explicitly:**
  - **Designated internal auditor:** An individual who has signed the Loom auditor agreement, is not the founder, and is not directly managed by the founder. Internal auditors may participate in `accepted` promotions under the interim governance rule.
  - **External auditor:** An individual with no financial, professional, or reporting relationship to the founder or the project. External auditors are required for `canonical` promotion.
- [ ] Define "independence" in relation to the research object before assigning auditor names to provenance records.
- [ ] Establish the distinction in the system:
  - **AI review** = machine-assisted checking.
  - **Human audit** = human examination.
  - **Promotion** = governance decision.

## Phase 6: Reconstruct Flint Using Real Sources

- [ ] Execute the first genuine vertical slice using *actual* documents, not placeholders.
- [ ] Workflow for the first source:
  `real document` → `real retrieval` → `real hash` → `real source record` → `real evidence passage` → `real claim` → `real verification event` → `real human audit`.
- [ ] Expand incrementally only after the first source is fully validated.
- [ ] **Legal/Copyright Recording:** Source retrieval and evidence extraction MUST record copyright/licensing status for every source. Full-document publication is permitted only where legally appropriate (public domain, explicit license, fair use documented); otherwise, excerpts, hashes, and archive links suffice. This requirement applies from Phase 6 forward, not deferred to Phase 13.

## Phase 7: Four-Person Adversarial Audit of Flint

- [ ] Deploy designated auditors to independently examine the Flint artifact.
- [ ] **Auditor Composition Rule:** The four auditors MUST be constituted per the Phase 5 governance addendum. At minimum, **not all four may be the founder or directly managed by the founder.** For the first Flint audit under the interim governance rule, at least two must be designated internal auditors; external auditors are strongly preferred but not yet mandatory until the Phase 5 addendum is ratified.
- [ ] Auditors must be able to reconstruct the full lineage:
  - Where did this claim come from?
  - What evidence supports/contradicts it?
  - Was the source actually retrieved?
  - Is the second source genuinely independent?
  - Did the claim stay within scope?
  - Who verified it? Who promoted it?
  - What would cause it to be demoted?

## Phase 12: Automation

- [ ] Introduce AI/automation strictly as subordinate tools to the evidence model:
  - Source discovery, extraction, duplicate detection, contradiction search, scope checks, provenance generation, validation, queue management.
- [ ] **AI subordination is enforced via the PL-307 Actor-Permission Matrix (§12), not just §18.** Specifically:
  - AI may set `epistemic_status: proposed` only.
  - AI may not set `promotion_stage` under any circumstances.
  - AI may not set `epistemic_status` above `proposed`.
  - AI-extracted evidence is capped at `source_backed` until human confirmation.
  - All AI actions must be recorded as provenance events with model identifier, version, and input object IDs.

## Governance Update: Retirement of the Solo-Founder Exception

Effective immediately, the "solo-founder governance exception" (which allowed a single individual to promote to `canonical` with a disclosure) is **retired as the default pathway**.

With a pool of 20+ available auditors, Loom will transition to a **Multi-Actor Audit Model**. The interim bootstrap rule (§Interim Governance above) governs the transition period until the Phase 5 addendum is ratified.
