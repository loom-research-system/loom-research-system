# PL-307 — Claims, Epistemic Status, Verification & Promotion Specification
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** **LOCKED**  
**Project:** Loom Research System  
**Effective:** 2026-09-10  

> **SCHEMA COMPLIANCE IS NECESSARY, BUT NOT SUFFICIENT, FOR RESEARCH CREDIBILITY.**  
> A valid object is not necessarily a true object. A claim becomes credible only through the underlying evidence, verification, provenance, review, and correction processes defined in this specification and the broader Loom system.

---

## 1. Purpose
PL-307 defines the state model governing Loom claims and establishes the distinction between:
1. workflow status;
2. epistemic status;
3. verification level; and
4. promotion stage.

These dimensions must not be collapsed into a single claim-status field.

PL-307 also defines:
* the relationship between evidence, observations, claims, and diagnostics;
* contradiction handling;
* promotion eligibility;
* lifecycle and revision management (including demotion and correction);
* validator requirements;
* diagnostic constraints; and
* provenance requirements.

---

## 2. Core Epistemic Rule
> **AI may propose. Evidence must establish. Humans may promote.**

AI-generated material is never authoritative merely because it was generated by a model. The Loom system must preserve the distinction between:  
**proposal → evidence → assessment → verification → governance decision**

No AI agent may independently promote its own output to the permanent Loom research record.

---

## 3. The Four Dimensions
Every claim MUST maintain four separate dimensions.

| Dimension | Question answered | Allowed values |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `status` | Where is the object in the workflow? | `proposed`, `under_review`, `system_flagged_for_review`, `completed`, `rejected`, `deprecated` |
| `epistemic_status` | What does the available evidence currently allow Loom to say? | `proposed`, `unknown`, `unresolved`, `contested`, `supported`, `known` |
| `verification_level` | How deeply has the claim been checked? | `none`, `source_backed`, `cross_verified`, `primary_verified` |
| `promotion_stage` | Where does the claim sit in Loom's governed research record? | `candidate`, `accepted`, `canonical` |

These fields answer different questions and must not substitute for one another.

---

## 4. Workflow Status
`status` describes operational progress only.
* **`proposed`**: The claim has been created but has not completed verification.
* **`under_review`**: Verification, contradiction analysis, or human review is in progress.
* **`system_flagged_for_review`**: A system-initiated state triggered by an evidence retraction cascade, staleness expiration, or validator failure. It routes the object to a human queue for `under_review`. It is distinct from human-initiated `under_review`.
* **`completed`**: The applicable verification and contradiction procedures have been completed. `completed` does **not** imply that the claim is supported.
* **`rejected`**: The claim has been determined unsuitable for further advancement. Rejection is a governance judgment (e.g., out of scope, redundant), not an evidentiary one.
* **`deprecated`**: The claim was previously used but is no longer considered valid or current. A deprecated claim MUST reference the applicable correction, replacement, or supersession record, OR provide a justification for outright rejection.

---

## 5. Epistemic Status
`epistemic_status` describes the substantive evidentiary condition of the claim.
* **`proposed`**: A human or machine has suggested the claim. No evidentiary conclusion has yet been reached.
* **`unknown`**: No relevant evidence was located within the defined search protocol as of the `current_as_of` date. Unknown is a legitimate research result.
* **`unresolved`**: Relevant evidence exists, but the available evidence is contradictory, incomplete, or otherwise insufficient to resolve the claim.
* **`contested`**: Credible evidence supports the claim while credible evidence also challenges it, and the contradiction remains unresolved. The contradiction MUST be recorded.
* **`supported`**: The available evidence supports the claim under the applicable Loom requirements. Limitations may remain. Supported does not mean absolute proof. If contradictory evidence exists, the contradiction MUST be `resolved`. **Verification floor:** `epistemic_status: supported` requires `verification_level` of `cross_verified` or stronger.
* **`known`**: A deliberately restricted status indicating that high-quality evidence establishes the claim under Loom's current standards. `known` MUST NOT be used as an ordinary synonym for `supported`. Unless otherwise specified by a domain-specific methodology, `known` requires: `cross_verified` or stronger; no unresolved contradiction; explicit `primary_applicability`; required primary examination where applicable; human review; and documented scope limits.

---

## 6. Verification Level
Verification level describes the depth of checking performed.
* **`none`**: No substantive verification has occurred.
* **`source_backed`**: At least one retrieved source contains evidence that appears to support the claim. A URL discovered through search is insufficient.
* **`cross_verified`**: The claim has been examined against a minimum of 2 independent sources. **Independence definition:** A source is independent only if it is independent from (a) the subject of the claim, and (b) every other source counted toward the same verification. Two sources sharing a common origin (wire report, syndication, common underlying dataset, derivative reporting) are NOT independent regardless of URL count.
* **`primary_verified`**: The relevant primary documentation has been examined where an applicable primary source exists. `primary_applicability` is a mandatory field with no silent default. It must be explicitly set to: `applicable`, `not_applicable`, or `destroyed_or_inaccessible`. This ensures Loom does not confuse the absence of evidence (e.g., destroyed documents) with researcher failure. `primary_verified` MUST NOT be claimed where applicable primary documentation exists and has not been examined.

---

## 7. Source Retrieval Requirement
A source is considered **retrieved** only when Loom has obtained usable documentary content (e.g., a local document, an API response, an archive snapshot, OCR text derived from a retrieved document, or a verified documentary excerpt).  
The following do NOT constitute retrieval: a search result, an unvisited URL, an AI-generated citation, a reference to a document that Loom cannot access, or a secondary description of an unavailable document.  
A source that has only been discovered MUST NOT independently serve as evidence.

---

## 8. Source Independence & Quality Assessment
Source independence means independence **from the subject of the claim and from other purportedly corroborating sources**. A source may be primary without being independent. Loom therefore evaluates: provenance, institutional relationship, shared underlying source, citation relationships, syndication, derivative reporting, and substantive independence. Source count MUST NOT be treated as equivalent to evidentiary independence.  
*Implementation default:* initial near-duplicate flag = ≥0.90 similarity at both document and passage levels.

---

## 9. Evidence Object
An `EVD` object represents documentary evidence extracted from a retrieved source. Evidence MUST preserve sufficient provenance to permit a reviewer to identify its origin.
```yaml
id: EVD-0001
type: evidence
source_id: SRC-0001
extracted_content: "..."
location: "page 14"
retrieved_at: "2026-09-10T10:00:00Z"
document_hash: "sha256:..."
extracted_by: "human" # or "model:..."
source_assessment: # MANDATORY for sources counted toward verification
  quality_rating: "high" # high, moderate, low, or unknown
  factors: ["directness: high", "bias_risk: low"]
  rationale: "Brief, auditable justification for the rating."
```
Evidence is not itself a claim. The evidence record should represent what the source contains before Loom interprets what that information means.

---

## 10. Observation Object
`Observation` is an explicit research object. It is a preliminary interpretation derived from evidence. **An `Observation` is not a `Claim` and cannot enter the promotion ladder.**  
The distinction is: `Evidence → Observation → Claim`  
An observation may state: *"The available records indicate that warnings were present before the intervention."*  
The claim may subsequently state: *"State officials received documented warnings concerning water quality before the emergency declaration."*

---

## 11. Promotion Stages
Promotion stages describe governance decisions regarding inclusion in the Loom research record: `candidate` → `accepted` → `canonical`.
* **`candidate`**: The claim has sufficient basis to warrant continued research or inclusion as a provisional research object.
* **`accepted`**: The claim has satisfied the applicable evidence and verification requirements and has been admitted to the active research record. **Verification floor:** `promotion_stage: accepted` requires `verification_level` of `cross_verified` or stronger.
* **`canonical`**: The claim has been explicitly admitted to the permanent Loom research record.

Promotion is a governance action. Meeting verification conditions creates **eligibility** for promotion. It does not automatically promote an object. Promotion MUST be recorded as a distinct event, including the actor, timestamp, and the exact text of any solo-founder disclosure.

---

## 12. Promotion Eligibility
The basic relationship is:  
`Epistemic assessment` + `Verification requirements` + `Provenance completeness` + `Applicable review` → `PROMOTION ELIGIBILITY` → `Human governance decision` → `Promotion`  
A claim MUST NOT become canonical solely because an automated validator determines that its evidence requirements have been met.

---

## 13. Promotion Matrix
| Epistemic status | Possible stage | Constraint |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `proposed` | `candidate` | Provisional only |
| `unknown` | `candidate` | Cannot advance as accepted knowledge |
| `unresolved` | `candidate` | Must remain explicitly incomplete |
| `contested` | `candidate` | Contradiction must be documented and unresolved |
| `supported` | `accepted`, potentially `canonical` | Requires `resolved` contradiction if contradictory evidence exists; requires ≥ `cross_verified` |
| `known` | `accepted`, potentially `canonical` | Restricted; requires additional requirements (no solo-founder exception) |

This matrix establishes **eligibility**, not automatic promotion.

---

## 14. Contradiction Handling
Contradiction MUST be represented structurally.
Allowed values for `contradiction_status`:
* **`none`**: No credible contradictory evidence has been identified.
* **`documented`**: Contradictory evidence has been identified and recorded, but substantive assessment has not yet been performed. This is a transient state during review.
* **`unresolved`**: A substantive contradiction has been assessed and remains unresolved.
* **`resolved`**: The contradiction has been evaluated and a documented resolution has been reached.

---

## 15. Contradiction Constraints
If contradictory evidence exists:
* `epistemic_status: known` is invalid when the contradiction remains unresolved.
* `epistemic_status: supported` is invalid when the contradiction remains unresolved.
* `contradiction_status` MUST accurately reflect the state of the conflict.
* Any resolution MUST identify the reasoning and evidence supporting the resolution.
A claim may remain `supported` despite contradictory evidence only where the contradiction has been explicitly assessed, resolved, and documented as not overturning the claim under the applicable methodology.

---

## 16. Claim Schema
Minimum claim representation:
```yaml
id: CLM-0001
type: claim
statement: "..."
request_id: REQ-0001
claim_type: "standard" # or "dispositive_document"
status: under_review
epistemic_status: contested
verification_level: cross_verified
promotion_stage: candidate
primary_applicability: applicable # MANDATORY, NO SILENT DEFAULT
current_as_of: "2026-09-10" # MANDATORY
search_protocol_id: "PROT-FLINT-001" # Required if epistemic_status is unknown
related_evidence:
- EVD-0001
contradictory_evidence:
- EVD-0011
contradiction_status: unresolved
contradiction_note: "State timeline provides an alternative explanation."
contradiction_resolution: null
scope:
  geography: "Flint, Michigan"
  exclusions: []
provenance:
  proposed_by: "human"
  proposed_at: "2026-09-10T15:32:00Z"
created: "2026-09-10"
updated: "2026-09-10"
```

## 17. Scope Integrity & Atomicity
Every substantive claim MUST have an identifiable scope. A claim MUST NOT become broader during the transition: `Evidence → Observation → Claim → Diagnostic` without an explicit evidentiary basis for the expansion.  
**Atomicity Rule:** A single claim MUST contain only one discrete, falsifiable assertion. Compound claims MUST be split into separate claim objects.  
A diagnostic MUST NOT imply a broader causal or institutional conclusion than its supporting claims permit.

---

## 18. Provenance and Actors
Claims MUST preserve the identity or role of actors involved in their creation and review (`proposed_by`, `extracted_by`, `verified_by`, `reviewed_by`, `promoted_by`).  
**AI Extraction Constraint:** If any **material** evidence supporting the claim was extracted by an AI system (including OCR transcription) and has not been independently confirmed by a human verification event against the original source document or image, the claim's `verification_level` MUST be capped at `source_backed`. A human verification event that independently confirms the AI-extracted evidence (by re-examining the source, not merely approving the AI output) removes this cap.

---

## 19. Verification Events
Verification history MUST be append-only. A verification event should record: `event_id`, `claim_id`, `actor`, `action`, `timestamp`, `inputs`, `result`, `method`, and `rationale`. Existing verification events MUST NOT be silently rewritten when the claim changes. Corrections and subsequent assessments MUST create new events.

---

## 20. Lifecycle, Revision & Staleness
All changes to the permanent record are governed by a unified **Revision Model**. Corrections, demotions, and deprecations are instances of a `REVISION` event.
* **20.1 Allowed Transitions:** Transitions must follow the lattice. Downward transitions (demotions) are always permitted. Upward transitions require meeting the conditions of the target state.
* **20.2 Evidence Retraction Cascade:** If an underlying source (`SRC`) is retracted or fails re-validation:
  1. All mapped `EVD` objects are marked `invalidated`.
  2. All `CLM` objects citing those `EVD` objects automatically transition to `status: system_flagged_for_review`.
  3. `verification_level` is recomputed downward. If it drops below the floor for the current `epistemic_status` or `promotion_stage`, those dimensions are demoted accordingly.
* **20.3 Verification Level Demotion:** `verification_level` is not static. It MUST be recomputed downward when underlying evidence is invalidated, or when an AI-extraction cap is applied retroactively.
* **20.4 Staleness and Recency:** Claims MUST include a `current_as_of` timestamp. Re-verification is triggered by time-based decay or event-based triggers (e.g., retraction of an underlying source).

---

## 21. Invalid-State Validation (Exhaustive Matrix)
The Loom validator MUST reject the following states:
* **Epistemic / Contradiction Violations:** `known` + `unresolved`; `supported` + `unresolved`; `contested` + `resolved` (stale state requiring reassessment).
* **Verification Floor Violations:** `supported` + `none`/`source_backed`; `accepted` + `none`/`source_backed`; `canonical` + `source_backed`.
* **Primary Verification Violations:** `primary_verified` + `not_applicable`/`destroyed_or_inaccessible`; `primary_verified` + no mapped primary `EVD`.
* **Promotion / Epistemic Mismatches:** `canonical` + `unresolved`/`contested`/`unknown`/`proposed`.
* **Workflow / Epistemic Mismatches:** `completed` + `proposed`; `completed` + `none`.
* **Diagnostic Violations:** `DIAG epistemic_status > min_lattice(material supporting CLM epistemic_status)`.
* **Cross-Verification / AI Violations:** `cross_verified` + < 2 independent sources; `cross_verified` (or higher) + AI-extracted material evidence without human confirmation event.

---

## 22. Validator Rule Structure
Every validation error MUST include: rule code, severity, affected object, reason, required remediation, and whether override is permitted.

---

## 23. Override and Correction
**Epistemic rules cannot have a convenient human override.** If the evidence state is wrong, the evidence state must be changed through the proper review path. `override_allowed: false` for all core epistemic violations (e.g., `EPISTEMIC-` prefixed rules). `override_allowed: true` is permitted ONLY for procedural exceptions (e.g., timestamp formatting, schema migration artifacts).

---

## 24. Demotion
Promotion is reversible. Demotion paths MUST be defined per dimension to prevent dimensional bleeding. A demotion event MUST preserve: previous state, new state, triggering evidence, reason, reviewer, timestamp, and correction reference.

---

## 25. Claim State Examples
* **Supported (with resolved contradiction):** `status: completed`, `epistemic_status: supported`, `verification_level: primary_verified`, `promotion_stage: accepted`, `contradiction_status: resolved`.
* **Contested:** `status: completed`, `epistemic_status: contested`, `verification_level: cross_verified`, `promotion_stage: candidate`, `contradiction_status: unresolved`.
* **Unknown:** `status: completed`, `epistemic_status: unknown`, `verification_level: none`, `promotion_stage: candidate`, `primary_applicability: destroyed_or_inaccessible`.

---

## 26. Schema Compliance Is Not Research Credibility
PL-307 explicitly rejects the proposition that a formally valid object is necessarily a credible research finding. Credibility additionally requires: real retrieved sources, authentic evidence extraction, appropriate source evaluation, meaningful contradiction search, accurate provenance, human review where required, transparent limitations, correction mechanisms, and adversarial testing.

---

## 27. Machine Enforcement Requirement
The rules in this specification MUST eventually be represented in machine-readable schemas and validation tests. Markdown documentation alone does not constitute enforcement.

---

## 28. Adversarial Testing Is a Release Requirement
Adversarial testing is not a future final phase. Every implementation of PL-307 MUST be tested against known failure conditions. A release that fails a critical epistemic test MUST NOT be labeled as satisfying PL-307.

---

## 29. Relationship to Other Loom Objects
The intended architecture is: `REQUEST → SOURCE → EVIDENCE → OBSERVATION → CLAIM → VERIFICATION → PROMOTION → ARTIFACT`.  
Diagnostics operate on the verified research layer: `CLAIMS + EVIDENCE → DIAGNOSTIC`.  
The promotion ladder is independent of the evidentiary pipeline.

---

## 30. Public Canonical Claims
Claims that are promoted to `canonical` and designated for public release are subject to additional governance constraints:
1. Strict separation between the actor who proposed the claim and the actor who promoted it; OR
2. Independent human review by a second party prior to publication; OR
3. A documented solo-founder exception, which requires a mandatory visible public disclosure stating: *"Single-reviewer artifact; no independent sign-off obtained."* (Note: This exception applies to `supported` and `canonical`, but **never** to `known`).

---

## 31. Normative Summary
Loom MUST preserve the following distinctions:  
**Workflow is not truth.**  
**Verification depth is not epistemic status.**  
**Epistemic status is not promotion.**  
**Primary source is not automatically independent.**  
**Source count is not corroboration.**  
**Contradiction documentation is not contradiction resolution.**  
**Schema validity is not research credibility.**  
**AI agreement is not evidence.**  
**Canonical is not infallible.**  
And above all: **AI may propose. Evidence must establish. Humans may promote.**
